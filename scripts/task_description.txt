Mutate a Python tuner for VarTODD/FastTODD policy search on the fixed matrix in
`helper.py`.

Goal: minimize reliable T-count rank. Matrix rank equals T-count, so lower is
better. Active instance: GF(2^16), n=48, initial rank 567. The hard frontier is
usually 420 -> 385.

---
## Program Contract

Every child must define:
- `class Evaluator(BaseEvaluator)` with `policy_mapping()` and `__call__(params)`.
- `entrypoint()` with no arguments.
- `entrypoint()` returning the selected evaluator's `get_best()`.

Use this import surface:
```python
import numpy as np

from helper import (
    BaseEvaluator,
    PARAM_ALWAYS_ACTIVE,
    ExplorationScore,
    FinalizationScore,
    PolicyScores,
    ActionSelection,
    ActionPool,
    SamplingBudget,
    SourcePool,
    TohpeSearch,
    ToddSearch,
    ZBucketSearch,
)
```

Basic shape:
```python
class Evaluator(BaseEvaluator):
    seeds = [42, 1337, 9001]

    def policy_mapping(self):
        ...

    def __call__(self, params):
        ranks = self.run(params, self.seeds)
        return float(np.quantile(ranks, 0.6))


def entrypoint():
    fun = Evaluator(path_name="init", max_depth=...)
    ...
    return fun.get_best()
```

If several evaluators are tried, return `get_best()` from the one that actually
found the best rank. Do not wrap or discard the active evaluator.

## Starts and Saved Paths

Use both search families across the population:
- `Evaluator(path_name="init", max_depth=...)` for ab initio builders.
- `Evaluator(path_name=<live_path>, margin=..., max_depth=...)` or
  `Evaluator(path_name=<live_path>, init_rank_thr=..., max_depth=...)` for
  saved-path refiners.

Saved path names must come from the current `## Live Path Store` prompt block.
If `rule=use_only_names_under_best_paths`, only `best_paths:` names are
loadable.

Saved-path refiners should beat the loaded rank. If they do not, change the
loaded path, restart threshold, margin, local optimizer, source mix, scoring, or
late-rank schedule.

Path format: `i<init_rank>_m<thr_rank>_f<final_rank>_<hash>`.

## Policy Objects

FastTODD searches actions `(z, y)`.
- `z` selects a bucket/nullspace.
- `y` is sampled in that nullspace.
- TOHPE is cheaper and searches a reduced space.
- TODD researches z buckets and can find actions TOHPE misses.

Grouped policy controls:
- `PolicyScores(exploration, final)`
- `ActionSelection(count, mode, temperature)`
- `ActionPool(final_size)`
- `SamplingBudget(one_hot, sparse, dense, sparse_max_weight)`
- `SourcePool(keep, reserve)`
- `TohpeSearch(sampling, pool, z_choices)`
- `ToddSearch(sampling, pool, actions_per_bucket, buckets)`
- `ZBucketSearch(min_buckets, max_buckets, temperature, random_fraction)`

Setters:
- `self.set_scores(...)`
- `self.set_action_selection(...)`
- `self.set_action_pool(...)`
- `self.set_tohpe_search(...)`
- `self.set_todd_search(...)`
- `self.set_widths(beam=..., actions=...)`
- `self.set_beamsearch_width(...)`
- `self.set_todd_width(...)`

Disable a source with `SourcePool(keep=0, reserve=0)` in that source object.

## Candidate Generation

`SamplingBudget` controls y-vector coverage independently for TOHPE and TODD:
- `one_hot="all"` tries every basis vector of weight 1.
- `one_hot=N` caps sampled one-hot vectors.
- `sparse=N` samples N vectors with weight in `[2, sparse_max_weight]`.
- `dense=N` samples N random vectors in the basis dimension.

Small nullspaces are easy to miss with tiny random budgets. For low dimensions,
`one_hot="all"` plus modest dense/sparse sampling is usually safer. Near
exhaustive coverage is about `2**dim - 1`, but very large coverage can make
final rank depend more on scoring quality than discovery.

`SourcePool(keep, reserve)`:
- `keep`: source-local retained candidates.
- `reserve`: minimum candidates from this source in the final merge when
  available.

`ActionPool(final_size)` should hold useful TOHPE/TODD candidates and reserves
without letting weak candidates dominate final selection.

`ActionSelection(count, mode, temperature)` controls final action choice in a
raw policy config. In evaluator runs, `set_todd_width(...)` or
`set_widths(actions=...)` supplies the materialized action count. Keep these
consistent unless the override is intentional.

`ToddSearch.actions_per_bucket` trades z diversity against deeper recall inside
one bucket.

`ZBucketSearch`:
- `min_buckets`: guaranteed number of z buckets researched when available.
- `max_buckets`: reserve-target search cap, not a hard search limit.
- `temperature`: Gumbel noise for bucket ordering.
- `random_fraction`: fraction of ordered buckets sampled uniformly.

TODD stops after `min_buckets` when its source pool has enough reserve actions.
`max_buckets > 0` caps how long TODD searches for that reserve target. If no
positive-reduction TODD action has been retained at the cap, TODD keeps scanning
until one action is found or all buckets are exhausted. `max_buckets=0` means no
reserve-target cap.

Only positive rank reductions are candidate actions. Zero and negative
reductions are discarded by candidate generation.

## Scoring

`ExplorationScore` ranks candidates during source pooling and merge.
`FinalizationScore` ranks final candidates and beam choices. Higher is better.

Features:
- `ExplorationScore(weights=[red, dim, bucket, vw, z], centers=[...], pow=...)`
- `FinalizationScore(weights=[red, dim, bucket, vw, z, tohpe], centers=[...],
  pow=...)`

Meanings:
- `red`: rank reduction.
- `dim`: nullspace basis dimension.
- `bucket`: z-bucket reduction potential.
- `vw`: y-vector Hamming weight.
- `z`: z-vector Hamming weight.
- `tohpe`: final-candidate TOHPE lookahead dimension.

Score formula:
`sum_i weights[i] * abs(feature[i] - centers[i]) ** pow`

Features are normalized to `[0, 1]`. With center `0`, positive weights reward
larger values and negative weights penalize larger values. Nonzero centers can
target medium dimensions, weights, or bucket potential.

Nonzero final `tohpe` weight costs extra state application and TOHPE-basis
recomputation. Use it only when this lookahead helps final choice.

Late success is not always greedy max reduction. Good scoring may preserve
future nullspace quality, source balance, or z diversity.

## Schedules and Parameters

Every grouped policy value can be constant or rank-scheduled:
- `setter(value)`
- `setter(ranks, values)`
- `setter(ranks=..., values=...)`

Ranks and values must have equal length. Thresholds are high to low. The first
value is fallback; lower thresholds override it after current rank crosses below
that threshold.

`map_par(mapping, thr=PARAM_ALWAYS_ACTIVE)` controls optimizer parameter
activity, not policy rank scheduling. The parameter is active only when
`init_rank < thr`.
- Omit `thr` or use `PARAM_ALWAYS_ACTIVE` for knobs that should stay tunable in
  saved-path refiners.
- Use lower thresholds to freeze early-rank knobs during late refinement.


Prefer compact parameterizations unless evidence justifies a larger optimizer
space.

## Optimizer and Runtime

Metric priority:
1. valid execution,
2. lower reliable final rank,
3. lower fitness for comparable rank behavior,
4. lower runtime only when rank/fitness are comparable or runtime is excessive.

Runtime around 1500-3000 seconds is normal for serious candidates. Around 3400
seconds indicates soft timeout risk.

Runtime scales with optimizer evaluations, seed count, beam width, action width,
source pools, final pool, TODD z budget, y sampling, depth, and expensive final
lookaheads.

Use pymoo wrappers for optimizer families such as PSO, CMAES, DE, and
PatternSearch-style local refinement. Cheap simple scouts are fine.

Useful patterns:
- few-seed scout, then focused refinement;
- compact global search from `init`, then `set_up_new_init`;
- saved-path refiner active around loaded rank and 420-385;
- TOHPE-heavy, TODD-heavy, and balanced source-mix variants;
- late-rank local search with fewer active parameters and stronger TODD recall.

Fixed seed lists make comparisons cleaner. A practical objective can early
reject on 1-3 seeds and fully evaluate promising candidates on 3-6 seeds.

## Reading Evidence

Use `path_policy_groups` with its `policy={...}` profile.

Important fields:
- `ranks`, `steps`
- `red_mean`, `red_max_mean`
- `dim_mean`, `dim_max`
- `bucket_mean`, `bucket_max`
- accepted TOHPE/TODD counts
- `accepted_min`
- `researched_z_mean/max`
- `accepted_per_z_mean/min/max`

Interpretation:
- High z research with low accepted_per_z: sparse z search or weak y sampling.
- Many accepted candidates but poor rank: final scoring, source merge, or beam
  choice is likely wrong.
- TOHPE success early does not prove TOHPE is enough late.
- TODD recall can help late, but high z budget without rank progress is waste.
- Wide rank quantiles indicate seed sensitivity.
- High `best_seen_times` with flat rank suggests saturation; mutate structure.

`search_stat` shows when optimizer improvements happened. Early best then flat
means change regime or restart. Improvements near the end suggest more budget
or a scout/refine split in that regime.

## Mutation Priorities

Make a concrete testable claim. Good levers:
- rank bands and active knobs per band;
- TOHPE/TODD mix, keep, and reserve;
- one-hot/sparse/dense sampling, especially in small nullspaces;
- TODD min z budget and actions per bucket;
- scoring weights, centers, and pow;
- action count, selection mode, and beam width;
- saved path, margin/rank threshold, and max_depth;
- optimizer family, eval budget, bounds, seeds, and local refinement;
- staged `set_up_new_init` after finding a good path.

When many children converge to one shape, introduce a structural alternative:
different source mix, schedule breakpoints, optimizer, saved path, or scoring
center model.
